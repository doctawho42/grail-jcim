\begin{abstract}
Recall in metabolite-structure prediction turns on two choices made outside the model: how a
returned structure is judged to match a known metabolite, and how many structures a system may
return. Under their default settings the systems compared here emit between \numWholeemitMin{} and
\numWholeemitMax{} structures per substrate, so their published recall figures sit on different
scales. No two of them can be read against each other. We release what puts them on one scale: the
per-substrate predictions of \numArmsTotalWord{} systems, \numComparatorsColumnsWord{} published
predictors and two configurations of a rule-grounded system of our own (GRAIL), frozen on one set
of \numWholeN{} drug-like substrates. The harness that scores them under five matching criteria
and nine output budgets is released with them. On that grid the output budget reorders the field.
SyGMa's \numSygmaRules{} curated rules, against this bank's \numBankRules{}, recover more than any
other method at every budget to five. At the widest budgets the ordering follows how much each
system emits, and holding every system to its own realised list length leaves
\numMatchedSurviveWord{} wide-budget lead of \numMatchedComparatorsWord{} standing. The matching
criterion moves a verdict at \numCritDefensibleMovedWord{} budget of \numCritBudgetsWord{}.
We also bound what a bank of this kind can reach. One application of the whole
\numBankRules{}-template bank recovers \numCeilingCoverage{} of known metabolites in a single
step. Of the \numCeilingUncovered{} it misses, \numCeilingNovel{} need a transformation type the
bank does not hold, \numCensusOnceSharePct{} of them in types the test split's annotation records
exactly once. That tail is a property of the sources this field mines, and it points to human
radiolabelled ADME studies as the corpus to mine next.
\end{abstract}
